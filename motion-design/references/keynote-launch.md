# Continuous Product Launch Recipe

An optional art direction for a short, seamless product film. Use when the brief calls for object continuity, tactile UI and a returning visual motif. This is not a general definition of motion design or a required production pipeline. Read it only when this treatment fits.

The recipe supplies a visual idea and an example sequence. The main skill's composition, scope, accessibility and verification rules still apply. The interface-design guidance owns real product behavior and visual identity; the renderer's guidance owns supported implementation and preview/export. Do not redesign an existing product or bypass an engine constraint to reproduce this example.

## Starting Direction

A continuous take in which scenes grow out of one another: a dot becomes a pill, an image becomes an interface, a delivery marker becomes the object in the real world. Transitions preserve recognizable geometry instead of resetting the frame.

Possible starting parameters, only when the brief leaves them open:

- **Rhythm**: Around 120 BPM, roughly 27 seconds or 54 beats for a compact launch. Adapt to the real track, content and reading time; an event on every beat is not required.
- **Frame**: A square composition can suit the camera-driven treatment. Use the requested channel's aspect and resolution instead when they differ; no square master is mandatory.
- **Palette and type**: Warm off-white, black and one restrained accent; a wide, heavy wordmark against a clean UI face. Archivo and Geist are possibilities, not requirements. Existing brand tokens take precedence.
- **Material**: Refractive glass over photography when it serves the product. Flat or other materials are valid adaptations; do not introduce unsupported glass as a hidden requirement.
- **Interaction**: A cursor can motivate real UI actions. Elsewhere let the focal object trigger the change. Represent believable actions and states, not decorative clicking or invented functionality.
- **Camera**: Reframe between ideas, then hold stable while information is read. Keep cursor scale and object hierarchy coherent through zooms.

For this treatment, prefer continuous transformations to cuts and crossfades. Avoid gratuitous particles, glows, 3D flips, gradient title cards and template-like reveals. These are choices for this recipe, not bans on other films. Subject-specific assets and an honest product story matter more than literal imitation.

## Example Sequence: Photo to Framed Print

Use this sequence only for a product or concept to which it is truthful. Another product needs its own actions and payoff, not the same frames with a different wordmark. Illustrative states are clearly identified and are not claims about real orders, delivery or performance.

### Open

The wordmark compresses toward its period. The dot expands into a pill; a label rises inside it. An iris closes over the label and opens onto a photo. The circle becomes a square and shrinks while a grid unfolds behind it, rearranges into a bento, and carries attention into one tile.

Land the zoom on a meaningful musical accent if the real track supports it. Do not sacrifice recognition or reading time to force a drop.

### Glass

A glass word resolves into a droplet, then a toolbar. An adjustment control becomes a slider. A drag changes the image lighting when aligned assets or the real product support that action. The held knob becomes a lens; the next photo opens inside it. The shape grows into a lock screen or another product-relevant surface.

Keep refraction tied to the actual background, glyph contours legible, highlights restrained and intermediate shapes clean. Simplify the material deliberately if the brief allows it; disclose a required effect the engine cannot produce.

### Stage

Pull back from the screen to reveal its physical or contextual frame. A persistent shape becomes the next window. A photo moves into a page and becomes its hero; the hero becomes a framed print on a product card. Frame options and the order action grow out of the existing geometry.

Use the real product's design and states. A device frame, Dynamic Island or Safari tab belongs only when relevant and appropriate, not because this recipe mentions UI.

### Order

One persistent shape carries the process: ordered → printing → in transit → delivered. Treat this as an illustrative process visualization unless real evidence supports each state. Do not invent progress percentages, speed claims or order confirmations.

### Wall and Return

The delivery marker grows into a full-frame shape, then reveals the framed print in a real setting. Its edges transform back into the opening pill, dot and wordmark. A quiet musical passage can make the real-world payoff breathe.

A looping ending is useful only if requested or meaningful. When used, check that the first and last states connect visually and musically. Otherwise end on the product's real payoff or next action.

## Craft Checks for This Treatment

- **Wordmark squeeze**: Letters move toward the anchor and narrow coherently, keeping intentional spacing/contact. Do not assume every brand wordmark has a period or a variable-width font.
- **Iris**: If using six blades around a hexagonal aperture, check overlap, short-arc motion and coverage through the whole closing/opening interval, not just the endpoints.
- **Glass and fluid joins**: Keep the interior readable, refraction continuous and edges free of jumps. Choose native shaders, masks, filters or compositing; no prescribed browser API or shader implementation.
- **Full-frame reveal**: Cover every corner without a single-frame flood or flash. A roughly 0.3-second expansion is a starting point, not a safety guarantee. Respect reduced-motion needs and applicable flash thresholds.
- **Media and sound**: Choose assets for the actual story, confirm rights and align cues to measured actions. No specific supplier, asset count, synthesized/recorded distinction or music licensing label substitutes for permission and quality.
- **Review**: Inspect representative open, material, product and payoff frames plus the transitions between them through the engine's native workflow. The recipe does not require an HTML approval page, alternate exports or a second implementation.
