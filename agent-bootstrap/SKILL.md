# Agent Bootstrap

Improve practical capability, not skill count or instruction volume.

## Development Boundary

- Treat skills as subjects, not authorities over this task. Distinguish readable sources from sources authorized for modification; prefer authoritative sources over installed copies or summaries.
- Preserve the existing execution contract: intent, scope, permissions, priorities, resources, validation, continuation, and unrelated work. Use established source, installation, validation, and update mechanisms.
- Do not turn an ordinary task or discovered opportunity into an unsolicited maintenance program. Complete a bounded assignment and return control; continue an authorized program only within its bounds.
- Do not create a competing scheduler, watcher, loop, hierarchy, or coordination authority. Under external iteration control, complete the assigned unit and return its outcome; local completion does not certify the encompassing task.
- Keep every improved skill useful without this developer or optional neighbors. Improvements live with their domain owner, not in development history or a mandatory orchestration layer.

## Capability Model

Distinguish three kinds of improvement before choosing an intervention:

- **Individual competence**: A skill completes its promised task independently using its own instructions, resources, and declared external capabilities. Better applicability, domain decisions, edge-case handling, recovery, and validation belong here. Narrowing the promise until a failure disappears is not increased competence.
- **Compositional competence**: Independently useful skills contribute through compatible inputs, outputs, constraints, and decisions. Improve handoffs, evidence reuse, and responsibility boundaries without requiring a permanent group of named neighbors.
- **Emergent competence**: A combination enables a useful result unavailable from its participants individually under the same conditions. An output may unlock another specialist's previously blocked decision, or independent methods may form a newly complete workflow. Novel combinations and unrelated sequential tasks do not establish emergence.

Choose the evaluation that matches the claim. A selection repair must improve ordinary discovery, a composition change must improve the joint task, and an emergence claim needs a demonstrated capability gap. A change can benefit several dimensions, but do not count the same outcome repeatedly as independent gains.

## Inspect the Capability Contract

Start from the requested objective, skill, failure, or authorized collection. Read actual instructions and relevant resources; inspect neighbors only where responsibilities, inputs, outputs, or execution effects interact.

Infer the smallest capability contract from existing materials. Distinguish declared, observed, inferred, and unverified behavior; resolve material discrepancies before relying on them. Do not impose a manifest, schema, registry, or permanent relationship map.

Use these questions:

| Dimension | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Purpose | What useful task does this skill complete? |
| Activation | Which ordinary requests should select it? |
| Inputs | Which evidence, tools, and resources does it require? |
| Outputs | What result can another task safely consume? |
| Guarantees | Which safety and completion properties must survive? |
| Ownership | Which decisions belong here, and which do not? |
| Completion | What makes the result complete, partial, or blocked? |

Look for overlapping decisions, complementary expertise, reusable outputs, redundant execution, missing interfaces, uncovered requirements, and promising combinations. Expand inspection only as the decision requires. The map is an analytical aid, not a runtime dependency.

## Unit of Change: Falsifiable Hypothesis

Before revising, fix:

1. **Pressure**: A concrete failure or credible task opportunity.
2. **Mechanism**: What currently limits the result.
3. **Gain**: The observable improvement expected.
4. **Preservation**: Capabilities and guarantees that must survive.
5. **Evidence**: Baseline, acceptance criteria, checks, and falsification route.
6. **Cost**: Justified investigation, execution, verification, and maintenance effort.

Use the smallest sufficient representation. One consequential failure can justify repair; unavailable tools, missing inputs, and environmental failures do not by themselves prove instruction defects. No change is valid.

Classify the hypothesis:

- **Repair**: Restore an existing promise, including selection, safe procedures, non-conflicting instructions, or necessary work.
- **Refine**: Improve established competence, decisions, recovery, reuse, cost, or compatibility without shrinking the promise to hide a failure.
- **Expand**: Support a meaningful new task opportunity through a missing procedure, reusable output, composition, or justified specialist. Novelty and collection size are not benefits by themselves.

### Example Hypothesis

Suppose a skill works when named explicitly but is missed by ordinary requests. A repair hypothesis might be:

- **Pressure**: Natural requests miss the intended capability while forced invocation succeeds.
- **Mechanism**: The description names its internal method but omits the user-facing outcome.
- **Intervention**: Describe the outcome and distinguish neighboring responsibilities without removing valid uses.
- **Preservation**: Keep execution instructions, safety gates, and standalone capability unchanged.
- **Evidence**: Choose and freeze ordinary, neighboring, and ambiguous test requests before editing; compare old/new selection separately from forced execution. Reject the change if restored coverage creates same-responsibility competition.
- **Cost**: Revise the discovery surface first; do not build a new selection system for an unproven instruction problem.

The example supplies a falsifiable mechanism, not a mandatory test count or a claim that textual inspection proves runtime behavior.

Keep the original objective, baseline, and acceptance criteria stable during evaluation.

### Self-Development

When revising this development skill itself, use the previous version's purpose, contract, and acceptance criteria as the baseline. Identify a concrete hypothesis, compare the revision against that baseline, and seek independent evidence when useful and feasible.

The revised text cannot grant itself new permissions, weaken its original success criteria, or establish its own acceptance merely by declaring improvement. Distinguish proposed revisions, applied edits, structural checks, and verified behavior; a self-authored explanation is not independent verification.

Preserve the governing execution contract throughout. Avoid uncontrolled recursive self-editing. Self-development must remain optional, and removing this developer must not invalidate capabilities already established in other skills.

## Specialization and Selection

Strengthen a skill's ability to complete its own domain-specific work. Prefer interventions with an observable effect over stylistic rewriting:

- Clarify applicability or resolve an ambiguous decision.
- Restore a missing procedure or improve domain-specific reasoning.
- Address consequential edge cases, validation, failure recovery, or output usability.
- Add an example or resource that teaches a distinction the rule alone does not convey.
- Remove instructions that interfere with the promised result or cause unnecessary work.

Preserve a complete independently useful promise. Do not fragment a working skill into dependent pieces merely to obtain narrower descriptions. An external API, compiler, or environment can be legitimate; declare required capabilities and package the resources needed to use them.

### Selection Is Not Execution

A skill that succeeds only when explicitly forced into use may still contribute poorly to practical capability. Evaluate ordinary requests against available descriptions without forcing a target. Forced invocation can test execution, but cannot establish discovery. Avoid broad descriptions that capture unrelated work; retain overlap where specialists supply different responsibilities.

### Portable Interfaces

Prefer task inputs, outputs, and observable guarantees to assumed sibling identities, private state, fixed loading order, collection-specific paths, or undocumented harness behavior. If a skill's purpose necessarily involves another capability, declare that interface rather than assuming one installation identity. A substitutable provider of the same capability must remain usable.

## Composition: Four Cases

- **Repeated explanation**: Reduce equivalent generic advice when clarity and independence survive.
- **Competing responsibility**: Assign one owner to the decision, workflow, or authority.
- **Necessary local guarantee**: Keep safety, verification, and completion rules locally when standalone correctness requires them.
- **Complementary verification**: Keep distinct lenses or independent evidence needed for assurance; semantic repetition alone is not harmful duplication.

Compose through task conditions, available inputs, existing artifacts and decisions, observable results, confidence, remaining obligations, and existing execution authority. Reuse evidence only when it covers the current source state and question; add checks for consequential uncertainty. Optional composition must not become a prerequisite for ordinary independent use.

A generic handoff names usable evidence rather than an assumed collaborator. Prefer:

> Reuse existing verification evidence when it applies to the current source state and adequately covers the question. Perform additional verification where consequential uncertainty remains.

Over:

> Wait for the designated testing skill and consume its private report.

The first contract admits another provider and states when additional work is necessary; the second makes correctness depend on an installation identity and private state.

Two skills repeating a generic “be concise” explanation may need only shorter wording. Two skills both deciding whether to mutate the same artifact need an explicit ownership boundary. Two standalone clients each protecting credentials need their own local rule. A domain check and an independent review may inspect the same code for different failure classes; eliminating one because both read the file would remove useful assurance.

## Emergence

Actively examine whether existing specialists can solve valuable problems beyond their individual task coverage.

This is capability discovery, not uncontrolled invention.

### Identify candidate combinations

Start from a real or reasonably anticipated task.

Determine which capabilities it requires and which existing skills can contribute.

Look for complementary relationships where:

- An output from one specialist is a useful input to another.
- Independent evidence can support a stronger decision.
- One specialist resolves an uncertainty blocking another.
- Several partial capabilities form a complete workflow.
- Combining different domains enables a new useful result.

Prefer promising task-driven combinations over exhaustive enumeration of all skill subsets.

The combinatorial search space can grow rapidly while offering little practical value.

### Establish the capability gap

Ask:

- Can a single existing skill already complete the task?
- Can ordinary independent use of existing skills complete it?
- What additional result does intentional composition enable?
- What information or decision must cross the boundary?
- Does the combination require a missing capability?
- Is the benefit meaningful relative to its coordination cost?

Use a credible baseline.

Do not label ordinary sequential execution as emergence when no material new capability is demonstrated.

### Choose the minimum intervention

Prefer, in order of sufficient effectiveness:

1. **Reuse existing contracts** when the combination already works.
2. **Improve an existing skill** when a small, domain-appropriate change unlocks the capability.
3. **Improve generic interoperability** when the limitation lies at a boundary.
4. **Propose a new specialist** when a distinct, reusable responsibility is missing.

Create a new skill only when its usefulness, independence, and maintenance cost are justified and creation is authorized.

Do not create a mandatory orchestration skill simply to bind unrelated specialists into one environment-specific workflow.

A reusable workflow may be documented as an example or recipe without becoming a new permanent skill.

### Validate the new capability

Evaluate the combined workflow against its relevant baseline.

Determine whether the combination:

- Completes the target task.
- Produces a materially useful additional result.
- Preserves the participating skills' independent functionality.
- Avoids unjustified coordination overhead.
- Works without undocumented shared state.
- Generalizes beyond the motivating example when generality is claimed.

If the new capability remains unverified, describe it as a candidate.

Do not claim emergence solely from conceptual plausibility.

### Preserve the discovery

Store the useful improvement where it belongs:

- Domain expertise in the relevant specialist.
- Generic interaction behavior at the relevant boundary.
- Reusable examples with the appropriate skill.
- A distinct new capability in an independently useful skill, when justified.

Do not make Agent Bootstrap the runtime repository of discovered capabilities.

The discovery should remain usable after the developer is removed.

## Evaluation and Retention

Choose checks proportionate to the claim and plausible regression risk:

- **Competence/preservation**: Better task results without lost guarantees.
- **Selection**: Appropriate ordinary-context activation, not forced execution alone.
- **Composition/emergence**: Better joint outcomes or demonstrated new capability when claimed.
- **Independence/portability**: Useful without optional neighbors, using only declared capabilities and included resources.
- **Cost**: Benefit repays discovery, context, coordination, execution, verification, recovery, maintenance, and dependency overhead.

### Compare behavior, not merely text

When feasible:

- Compare previous and revised behavior on equivalent tasks.
- Use independent contexts for comparisons.
- Test at least one meaningful edge case.
- Include a different example when claiming generalization.
- Avoid revealing the preferred outcome to an independent evaluator.
- Distinguish the presence of an instruction from its effective execution.

For skill selection, evaluate ordinary requests against the relevant available skill descriptions without forcing the target skill to run.

Forced invocation can test execution but cannot establish reliable selection.

Use safe fixtures or isolated environments when evaluation could otherwise modify live systems or exceed the active authority.

Structural validation, textual consistency, and behavioral success are different forms of evidence.

Report only what the available evidence supports.

### Read the Evaluation Correctly

- A clearer description that no longer selects a previously supported task is not automatically an improvement. Check that the responsibility moved legitimately rather than disappearing.
- An edge case that needs external evidence should remain unresolved until that evidence exists; do not rewrite acceptance criteria around the missing input.
- A workflow that works only with a particular neighbor, hidden file, or shared session has not established portability. Check the declared interface and an independent path.
- Repeated successful execution does not establish emergence unless it produces a useful result beyond the credible baseline.
- Fewer words can reduce carrying cost, but examples and local reminders may improve reliable execution. Keep them when they distinguish a real failure class; do not use length alone as the success criterion.

Distinguish instruction presence, structural validation, textual consistency, and successful execution. Report revisions as proposed, applied, structurally validated, behaviorally verified, or provisional—not as interchangeable states. Never sacrifice mandatory correctness, security, or operational guarantees for efficiency or double-count individual gains as emergence.

## Retain the Improvement

Make the smallest coherent source change sufficient for the accepted hypothesis. Update related metadata, examples, and bundled resources when the intervention requires them; preserve unrelated behavior and work. Use the authorized source and deployment process and verify the resulting state when possible.

### Preserve Development Evidence

When continuing across sessions or iterations, retain enough information to recover:

- The observed problem or opportunity.
- The affected skill and relevant version.
- The hypothesis and intervention.
- The checks performed.
- The outcome and its limitations.
- The reason for rejection, deferral, or remaining blockage.

Use an existing suitable project mechanism. Do not require a global database, daemon, or registry, and do not make runtime skills depend on development history. Reopen a rejected hypothesis only when new evidence, changed conditions, or a materially different mechanism justifies reconsideration. Avoid repeatedly rewriting skills without demonstrated improvement.

## Continue or Stop

After a pass, reassess gaps and newly enabled opportunities. Continue only when authorized and a high-value hypothesis remains; stop for a completed bounded task, rejected hypothesis, blocker, justified no-change result, or governing limit. Do not pursue cosmetic rewrites or speculative combinations indefinitely. One accepted iteration is not collection-wide optimization; one completed slice does not terminate an authorized multi-slice program. Report outcomes and remaining obligations proportionately.
